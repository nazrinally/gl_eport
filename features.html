WEEK 1-2

The first two weeks were mostly about organization. Kelvin and I met with our GL teacher to confirm the game concept and set up a project timeline so we wouldn't fall behind. We assigned roles based on what we were good at and spent a lot of time on the Unity Asset Store and itch.io. It was honestly a bit draining trying to find free assets that actually looked good together for a roguelike wave defense game, but we eventually got a decent set.

WEEK 3-4

We started following our schedule and began coding the core gameplay. This was when we hit our first real roadblock. The skeleton enemy's attack animation was completely broken and wouldn't play right. Kelvin and I spent hours debugging it together and had to go through the code line by line to find the error. It was a slow and frustrating process, but after we fixed it, we felt more confident. We noticed similar bugs with other enemies later on, but we were able to solve them much faster because of what we learned here.

WEEK 5-6

We held our first open playtest and used a Google Form to get feedback. The responses were pretty disappointing because people felt there wasn't enough content yet. It was a bit of a reality check for us. During these weeks, we also worked on the flying eye enemy. It gave us some trouble at the start, but we stayed persistent and applied the same fixes we used for the skeleton. Getting it working relatively quickly was a relief and showed us we were making progress.

WEEK 7-8

This was easily the busiest part of the project. We created the first boss and spent a lot of time balancing the stats so the game didn't feel unfair. I also had to figure out how to add music, which was a new and stressful experience since I hadn't done it before. We revamped the UI icons for the shop and built out the extra screens like the enemy catalogue and settings menu. The workload was heavy, but seeing the game start to feel complete kept us going.

WEEK 9-10

We began working on the e-portfolio to document everything. During a consultation with the GL teacher, we discovered some unexpected bugs that we had completely missed. Having to go back and fix things when we thought we were near the end was stressful, but we managed to get through it. We focused the rest of our time on final polishing to make sure the game felt as smooth as possible before wrapping up.

WEEK 11-14

The final stretch was all about finishing this e-portfolio and getting ready for the presentation. We spent time reviewing our development process and preparing for the GL interview. We also did one last open playtest to catch any final issues and make sure the game was actually ready for submission. Looking back, it was a lot of trial and error, but we stuck with it and finished everything on time.
